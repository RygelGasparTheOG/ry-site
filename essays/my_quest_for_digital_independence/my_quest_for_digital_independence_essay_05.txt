My Quest for Digital Independence — The Aftermath

Over these series of essays I have written, I’ve documented my entire journey of replacing cloud services in my life, from replacing Netflix and Spotify, to even replacing AI tools such as Claude, that I used as a puppet in the original version of essay 4 to not sound crazy. Now it’s time to talk about some things. There were some parts of my journey, where I would get stuck, and take days trying to fix something, by deciding to suffer in ASM, or typing 25,498 examples in JSON to make Laude truthfully smart, but overall I conquered it.

This journey has been massive, I don’t use a lot of the cloud services that I used before, like Netflix. Sure, I didn’t manage to replace most of the other services, such as a voice assistant, because I was practically limited as to where I could run my own services on, well I wasn’t that limited and you had just seen me lying again, I wasn’t that limited because turns out, that if I used the client as the STT and TTS processing, I would have replaced Gemini as my voice assistant. I have hosted most of my replacements on my old phone, and that’s okay for me, it has been super reliable for me, considering on how underpowered the hardware is, in fact the old phone I’m using to host RyCord and Laude AI, a Huawei Y7a, is unreliable for moderate gaming for me, so I used it to play lightweight games only. So I turned it into my own server

In this entire journey, I learned perseverance, to persevere through difficulties. I learned bravery, to fight for my own privacy. I learned to be resourceful, resourceful to use what I have, even though how old nor underpowered it is. And that’s it, I hope I inspired you to do the same as to what I did, well maybe not use a phone to host your cloud service replacements.
